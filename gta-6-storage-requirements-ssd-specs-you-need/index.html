How to choose the right storage for GTA 6

When selecting storage for a title like GTA 6, you are balancing three main factors: capacity, speed, and interface.

Capacity vs. Speed

The game is expected to be one of the largest releases in recent history. While exact file sizes are unconfirmed, current trends suggest a base installation size that could range from 100 GB to over 150 GB, depending on resolution and texture packs. You need to account for this, plus space for updates, mods (on PC), and other games you play.

Speed is equally critical. Modern open‑world games rely on “streaming” data from the drive to the GPU and CPU constantly. If the drive cannot feed data fast enough, the game engine has to wait, resulting in load screens or visual pop‑in. NVMe (Non‑Volatile Memory Express) drives are the standard for this, significantly outperforming older SATA SSDs and traditional Hard Disk Drives (HDDs).

Interface Matters

For PC users, the interface speed is dictated by the M.2 slot in your motherboard. Look for PCIe 4.0 or PCIe 5.0 connections. PCIe 5.0 offers theoretical speeds that are double those of 4.0, which is ideal for future‑proofing. If you are using an external drive, ensure it supports USB 3.2 Gen 2 (10 Gbps) or higher. USB 2.0 or even basic USB 3.0 (5 Gbps) may be bottlenecks for a game of this scale.

Console Specifics

On PlayStation 5, you cannot play PS5‑optimized games from a USB external drive; that drive can only store the game files. To play GTA 6 (or any PS5 title) from expanded storage, you must install a compatible M.2 NVMe SSD in the console’s internal expansion slot. The drive must meet Sony’s speed requirements (typically a minimum sequential read of 5,500 MB/s).

On Xbox Series X and Series S, next‑gen optimized games such as GTA 6 require either the internal SSD or the official Seagate/Western Digital Storage Expansion Card plugged into the dedicated expansion port. Standard USB external drives can play Xbox One and backward‑compatible titles, but they cannot run Series X|S optimized games.

Common mistakes to avoid

Ignoring Thermal Throttling

High‑speed NVMe drives generate heat. If your PC case has poor airflow, or if you are using an external SSD without active cooling for long sessions, the drive may throttle its speed to prevent overheating. This can lead to inconsistent performance during long gaming sessions. Ensure your storage solution has adequate cooling or is used in a well‑ventilated area.

Underestimating Update Sizes

Game developers often release large patches after launch. If you buy a drive with just enough space for the base game, you may find yourself out of room within weeks. Always aim for at least 20 % more space than the expected initial install size.

Using HDDs for Active Gaming

While Hard Disk Drives (HDDs) are cheap, they are not suitable for modern open‑world games. The mechanical nature of HDDs means they cannot keep up with the rapid data requests of a game like GTA 6. You will experience significant stuttering and long load times. Use HDDs for backups, not for active gameplay.

Misunderstanding File System Limits

If you are using an external drive, ensure it is formatted correctly. NTFS supports volumes up to 8 petabytes, so the 2 TB partition limit is not a concern. For drives larger than 2 TB, exFAT is often used for cross‑platform compatibility, but check that your console or PC supports the chosen file system before formatting.

FAQ

Will GTA 6 run on a standard SATA SSD?

Technically, yes, but it will not be the optimal experience. SATA SSDs are limited to 6 Gbps speeds, which is significantly slower than the NVMe drives required for modern fast streaming. You will likely see longer load times and potentially more pop‑in. For a game of this scale, NVMe is strongly recommended.

How much space does GTA 6 actually take up?

Exact figures are not officially confirmed yet, but based on current market trends and the scale of previous titles, expect the base game to be between 100 GB and 150 GB. With updates and high‑resolution texture packs, this could easily push past 200 GB.

Can I play GTA 6 from an external USB drive on console?

On Xbox Series X and S, you cannot play Series X|S optimized games from a standard USB drive; you must use the internal SSD or the official expansion card. On PlayStation 5, you cannot play PS5 games from a USB external drive at all; you can only store them there. To play the game, you need to install it on the internal SSD or a compatible M.2 NVMe SSD in the expansion slot.

Is a 1 TB drive enough?

For GTA 6 alone, a 1 TB drive is sufficient if you do not plan to keep other large games installed. However, if you have a library of other AAA titles, 1 TB will fill up quickly. Consider a 2 TB drive for better long‑term flexibility.

Do I need a PCIe 5.0 drive?

No, you do not strictly need a PCIe 5.0 drive. PCIe 4.0 drives are more than fast enough for GTA 6. PCIe 5.0 drives are more expensive and run hotter, offering diminishing returns for gaming. Stick with a high‑quality PCIe 4.0 drive for the best value.

What if my drive fills up during the game?

If your drive fills up, the game will likely crash or fail to update. It is important to monitor your storage space regularly. You can also delete unused files or move other games to a secondary drive to free up space.

Are external SSDs durable enough for daily use?

Most modern portable SSDs are shock‑resistant and water‑resistant, making them suitable for daily use. However, they are not indestructible. Avoid dropping them or exposing them to extreme temperatures. For internal NVMe drives, ensure they are properly seated in the M.2 slot to prevent damage.

Can I upgrade my console’s internal SSD?

No, the internal SSDs in current‑generation consoles are soldered to the motherboard and are not user‑upgradable. You must rely on internal storage or use external expansion drives.

Is there a difference between “Gaming SSD” and standard SSDs?

“Gaming SSDs” are often just standard SSDs with marketing labels. However, some gaming‑focused brands include software for RGB lighting or performance monitoring. The core hardware is usually the same. Focus on the speed specs and reliability rather than the “gaming” label.

How do I check if my drive is fast enough?

You can use benchmarking software like CrystalDiskMark to test your drive’s read and write speeds. For GTA 6, look for sequential read speeds of at least 3,000 MB/s for NVMe drives. Anything slower may result in a less smooth experience.
