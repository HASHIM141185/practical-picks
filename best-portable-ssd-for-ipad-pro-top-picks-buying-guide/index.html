How to choose

Capacity

Portable SSDs for the iPad Pro are available from 500 GB up to 4 TB. Consider how much media you plan to store locally. If you mainly stream content, a 500 GB drive may be enough; for 4K video editing, 1 TB or more is safer.

Speed (Read/Write)

USB‑C SSDs can reach 1,050 MB/s read and around 1,000 MB/s write. Faster drives reduce import and export times for large files, which is especially noticeable when moving video clips between the iPad and the drive. Look for drives that advertise these numbers; write speed matters when you’re saving new footage directly to the SSD.

Durability & Size

If you travel frequently or work in rugged environments, choose a model with a rugged rating (e.g., IP55) or a metal enclosure that can survive drops. Smaller drives fit easily in a pocket, while larger capacities may be a bit thicker.

Price

Prices vary widely based on capacity and speed. A 500 GB drive can be found for a modest amount, while a 2 TB model with top‑tier speeds may cost several hundred dollars. Set a budget before you start comparing features.

Compatibility & Connection

All the drives listed use USB‑C, which is the native connector on the iPad Pro. iPadOS requires external drives to be formatted as exFAT for cross‑platform use; APFS is not natively supported for external storage on iPad. You can reformat the drive on the iPad or a Mac before first use. Some drives include optional encryption software, which can be useful for protecting sensitive files.

Warranty

A solid warranty (typically 3 years) gives peace of mind. Check the manufacturer’s policy for coverage of physical damage, data recovery, and whether the drive can be replaced if it fails.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying a drive with the wrong connector: Ensure the SSD uses USB‑C; older USB‑A or Thunderbolt models won’t connect directly to the iPad Pro.
  • Overlooking capacity needs: Underestimating how much space you’ll need can lead to frequent upgrades. Aim for at least 1 TB if you plan to store multiple 4K video projects.
  • Ignoring durability: A fragile drive may survive a few moves but can fail under regular travel conditions. Look for rugged ratings or metal housings if you’re often on the go.
  • Paying for speed you don’t use: If most of your work involves reading files rather than writing, a drive with extremely high write speeds may be unnecessary and cost more.
  • Skipping warranty checks: A short warranty can leave you exposed if the drive fails early. Prefer models with at least a 3‑year warranty.
  • Assuming the drive works out‑of‑the‑box: iPadOS requires external drives to be formatted as exFAT for cross‑platform compatibility. Reformatting may be needed before first use.

FAQ

Q: Can I use any USB‑C SSD with my iPad Pro? A: Yes, as long as the drive uses a USB‑C connector and is formatted as exFAT (or another iPad‑compatible file system). You may need to reformat the drive on the iPad or a Mac before first use.

Q: Do I need a special cable, or does the SSD come with one? A: Most SSDs include a short USB‑C to USB‑C cable. If you need a longer cable, make sure it’s a high‑quality, data‑capable cable that supports the drive’s speed.

Q: How fast will file transfers be compared to the iPad’s internal storage? A: Transfer speeds will be close to the drive’s advertised read/write rates, but real‑world times vary with file type, cable quality, and the specific iPad model. For example, moving a 10 GB video file could take roughly 10–20 seconds on a 1,050 MB/s drive, but actual times may be longer.

Q: Is data encryption available on these drives? A: Some models, like the Samsung T7 and SanDisk Extreme, offer hardware encryption or password protection via included software. Check the product description for these features if security is a priority.
