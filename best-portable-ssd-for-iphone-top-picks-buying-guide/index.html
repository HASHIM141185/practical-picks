How to Choose a Portable SSD for iPhone

1. USB-C and the cable you use

On iPhone 15 and newer models, the USB-C port handles data as well as charging, so a portable SSD can be plugged in directly. Older iPhones with a Lightning port generally can't record video to an external drive; they can only transfer files through a powered Lightning to USB 3 camera adapter.

The cable is the part people get wrong most often. The USB-C cable that ships in the iPhone box is designed for charging and transfers data at slow USB 2.0 speeds. For backups, a standard USB-C cable is usually fine. For direct ProRes recording, use a cable rated for 10Gbps (USB 3.2 Gen 2) and keep it short.

2. Formatting: exFAT or APFS

iOS can read drives formatted as exFAT or APFS. Most portable SSDs ship with exFAT, which also works with macOS and Windows, so it is the safest default. A drive formatted as NTFS will appear but won't accept new files from an iPhone until you reformat it on a computer.

3. Power draw

The phone powers the drive through its port. Portable SSDs designed for phones and cameras draw very little power. Mechanical hard drives and some high-performance desktop enclosures draw too much, causing errors, disconnects, or a sudden battery warning. Stick with a slim portable SSD built around a single, efficient controller.

4. Direct recording requirements

If you want to record 4K ProRes video to an external drive on an iPhone Pro model, Apple requires a USB 3.2 Gen 2 (10Gbps) connection and a drive with write speeds of at least 220MB/s. In practice, a drive that sustains 450MB/s or more gives you a much safer margin when the phone is also doing other work.

5. Capacity

Video eats storage fast. A 1TB drive gives you room for a working library, while 2TB is sensible if you shoot regularly and back up in the field. Whatever you choose, a drive is a working drive, not a permanent archive — keep a second copy somewhere else.

But it's worth knowing about the reliability concerns that have been publicly reported around certain SanDisk Extreme portable SSD models, including revisions commonly sold as Extreme V2 and Extreme Pro. Owners have posted reports of drives failing, and class-action lawsuits have been filed concerning these products.

That's a reason to slow down before buying, not a reason to skip the brand entirely. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Using the cable that came with the iPhone. It transfers data slowly and can fail during recording. Use the SSD's own cable or a dedicated 10Gbps cable.
  2. Buying a mechanical hard drive. Spinning drives need too much power, are fragile, and are far too slow for video recording. Choose an SSD.
  3. Pulling the cable out mid-transfer. Close the Files app or Camera app and wait for the activity light to stop before disconnecting, so a half-written file isn't left behind.
  4. Paying for 2000MB/s speeds you'll never use. Some drives advertise USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 speeds. iPhones top out around 1050MB/s, so the extra speed only matters if you also move files to a compatible laptop.
  5. Treating the SSD as your only copy. If the drive fails, the footage is gone. Keep a second copy on a computer, a second drive, or cloud storage.
  6. Ignoring the model revision. Product names repeat across generations. Check the model number and revision on the drive itself before comparing reviews or warranty coverage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I record video directly to an SSD on a base-model iPhone 15?

The iPhone 15 and 15 Plus use USB 2.0 data speeds, so external ProRes recording isn't supported on those models. You can still use an SSD for file transfers and photo backups through the Files app. Direct external recording requires an iPhone Pro model with a USB 3.2 Gen 2 or faster port.

How do I open files from the SSD on my iPhone?

Connect the drive, open the Files app, tap Browse, and select the drive under Locations.

Will an SSD drain my iPhone's battery?

Connecting a drive uses some power, but a low-power portable SSD has a modest impact during normal transfers. Continuous ProRes recording drains the battery faster, so keep a power bank nearby for long shoots.

Do I need a third-party app?

No. iOS handles external storage natively through the Files app, and the stock Camera app automatically routes ProRes video to a compatible drive on Pro models.

Is exFAT better than APFS for an iPhone SSD?

Both work. exFAT is the more portable choice because Windows and Android devices can read it too. APFS is Apple's own format and works well if the drive stays in an Apple-only setup. Whichever you pick, reformat the drive before recording so you know it's set up correctly.
